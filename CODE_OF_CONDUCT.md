# Code of Conduct

This project is run by the OpenSource Students Club (OSSC) at Don Bosco Institute of Technology.
Most contributors are students, and for many this is a first open source project. We want it to be a
place where people can learn, make mistakes and ask questions without fear.

## What we expect

- Be respectful and patient. Assume the other person means well.
- Give feedback on the code, not on the person. Explain the reason behind a review comment.
- Welcome newcomers. Nobody is too junior to ask a question or open a pull request.
- Give credit for other people's work, and own up to your own mistakes.
- Keep discussion on topic and in the open (issues, pull requests, the club channels).

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or jokes that target someone's gender, caste, religion, region, language,
  appearance, disability, or any other personal trait.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome advances.
- Sharing someone's private information without their permission. This project handles real student
  data, so this includes posting names, roll numbers, phone numbers, emails or payment screenshots
  in issues, pull requests, screenshots or chat.
- Deliberately disrupting the event or the site: fake registrations, spam, or testing attacks against
  the live site without permission from the maintainers.
- Pressuring or retaliating against someone who reports a problem.

## Where this applies

In this repository, in OSSC channels (Discord, WhatsApp, sessions in the lab), and anywhere you are
representing the project or the club.

## Reporting

If you see or experience something that breaks this code of conduct, tell an OSSC core team member,
in person or by direct message. Core team contacts and the club channels are listed at
[webuildnow.in](https://webuildnow.in). If the matter involves a core team member, go to the faculty
coordinator of the club or the event coordinator in the Department of Chemistry.

Reports are kept confidential. The person reporting will not be named without their consent.

## What happens next

The core team will look into every report and respond as fairly and quickly as it can. Depending on
what happened, the response can be a private word, a formal warning, removal of content, a temporary
break from the project, or removal from the project and the club's spaces. Serious matters are passed
on to the college.
